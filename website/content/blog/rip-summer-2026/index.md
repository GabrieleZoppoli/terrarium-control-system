This summer took seven plants. Five of them had arrived only this year; one had been here for nearly ten.

## *Haraella retrocalla*

<p class="species-facts">Taiwan, Ryukyu Islands · Großräschener Orchideen · nearly ten years</p>

The longest-held of the seven. A miniature orchid from Taiwan and the island of Iriomote, at the southern end of Japan. I bought it as *Haraella odorata*, an older synonym; Kew now places it in *Gastrochilus*. The plant fits on two fingers, and the flower is yellow-green with a fringed lip and a dark purple blotch in the middle.

## *Aldrovanda vesiculosa*

<p class="species-facts">Europe, Africa, Asia, Australia · Un Angolo di Deserto · March 2026</p>

The waterwheel plant: a rootless aquatic that floats just under the surface, with whorls of tiny snap traps along the stem. It is the only living species of its genus and the closest relative of the Venus flytrap. It lived in the small outdoor ponds, which dried out twice this summer.

## The ones without a photo

***Picea pungens* 'Glauca Globosa'.** A dwarf, rounded form of the Colorado blue spruce, in a 24 cm pot on the balcony since March 2020.

***Pinguicula immaculata*.** A miniature butterwort from Nuevo León, Mexico. It came from Diflora in March 2026 and lived on the misted shelves indoors.

***Pinguicula* 'Razzberry Blonde'.** A hybrid butterwort, from Giardino Carnivoro in May 2026.

***Phalaenopsis stobartiana* and *Phalaenopsis malipoensis*.** Two small species *Phalaenopsis* from Yunnan; *malipoensis* is named after Malipo county, and also grows in Vietnam. They came from Claessen and Celandroni in April 2026 and died on the shelves in September.

Every grower has a list like this, and most of the names on it are plants we wanted for a long time before we got them.
