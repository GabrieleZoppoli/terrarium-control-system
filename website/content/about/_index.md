I'm **Gabriele Zoppoli**, a clinician-scientist in oncology and hematology at the University of Genoa and, in the hours the rest of life leaves free, a keeper of highland carnivorous plants, miniature orchids and grey *Tillandsia*. The collection documented on this site has grown one accession at a time, mostly out of the pleasure of finding, the next morning, that a species which should be impossible to keep is still alive.

The systematic side of all this (the spreadsheet, the phylogenetic ordering, the open-source control loop) is a habit from the lab. Applied to plants that are not an experiment, it mostly comes down to paying attention and not losing what small growers have taken the trouble to send.

## The day job

Associate Professor of Internal Medicine at the University of Genoa and Molecular Tumor Board coordinator at *IRCCS Ospedale Policlinico San Martino*, with earlier stints at the NIH/NCI in Bethesda and at Institut Jules Bordet in Brussels. Current research is in liquid biopsy (cell-free DNA and single-molecule sequencing) for early diagnosis and longitudinal monitoring of breast cancer. Papers and grants are listed at [ORCID](https://orcid.org/0000-0002-1619-1708), [Google Scholar](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=Q5DfyqQAAAAJ), and the [UniGe directory](https://rubrica.unige.it/persone/UkBGXVtc).

## How it started

It began with a single *Dionaea muscipula* on a windowsill. Then *Nepenthes*, which looked ready to die for a whole year and then did not. Then *Heliamphora*, *Dracula*, miniature *Dendrobium*: species I had only seen in books, sent by small growers across Europe who still pack their plants by hand. A few years in it was clear that the windowsill, not the plants, was the limit, and the [highland cabinet](../highland/) came out of that. The census now stands at over 400 acquisitions in more than 90 genera, about 300 of them alive.

## Who does the work

The collection is mine on paper but two-handed in practice. **My mother-in-law** covers mist cycles and hand-watering when I'm at the hospital, lends her name to orders from vendors who only ship to one country, and is responsible for roughly half of the plants still alive.

None of it would work without the European carnivorous-plant and orchid growers: Andreas Wistuba for *Nepenthes* and *Heliamphora*; Vincenzo Castellaneta at Un Angolo di Deserto for *Pinguicula*; Giulio Celandroni in Pisa for orchids; Lieselotte Hromadnik in Kritzendorf, who still mails *Tillandsia* typed up on paper; and many other small growers whose names fill the acquisition records.
