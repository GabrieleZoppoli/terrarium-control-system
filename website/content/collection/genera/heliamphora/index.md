## Overview

*Heliamphora* are carnivorous pitcher plants endemic to the tepui summits of the Venezuelan and Guyana Highlands. They grow fully exposed on nutrient-poor sandstone at 1,800–2,800 m, where persistent cloud provides moisture and the pitchers trap small insects.

## Position in Terrarium

**Upper zone**: high light, directly under the LED array, the brightest position in the cabinet.

## Substrate

Akadama + long-fiber sphagnum, topped with a living *Sphagnum* moss layer. No standing water; humidity alone keeps the substrate moist.

## Cultivation notes

Nine accessions, all alive. The terrarium's diurnal swing (≈13.5 °C night, 22 °C day, 85–100 % RH) is inside the range *Heliamphora* want, with no further intervention. One well-tuned cabinet and a generic mix of akadama and long-fibre sphagnum replace the usual chillers and fans.

A few observations from provenance records:

- *H. minor* is represented by four clonal lines, including **var. pilosa (Auyán), Clone 3**, **Clone 4**, and the darkly pigmented **'Burgundy Black'**. They arrived as adult plants and have produced new pitchers every growth cycle since.
- *H. pulchella* is here in two clonal lines, from **Akopan Tepui** and **Amuri Tepui**. Under identical conditions the two differ markedly in pitcher proportions.
- Of the hybrids, **'Red Giant'** (*H. purpurascens* × *ionasii*) shipped juvenile on the first attempt and was replaced free of charge with adult-sized plants.
- **'Godzilla'** is the only juvenile in the group (clone AW-H_Godz), and the test of whether the highland routine works for young material as well as for rooted adults.

No losses to date. Nectar-spoon development and pitcher colour are both best on the plants directly under the LED pucks, the one position I rotate between accessions monthly.
