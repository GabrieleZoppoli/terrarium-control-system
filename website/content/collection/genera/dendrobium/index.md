## Overview

*Dendrobium* section *Oxyglossum* includes some of the most spectacular highland orchids in cultivation. Native to the mossy forests of Papua New Guinea at 2,000--3,000 m elevation, they produce clusters of blue-purple flowers, a colour rare in orchids and almost unique to this section.

They evolved independently of Andean cloud-forest orchids such as *Dracula* and *Masdevallia*, and need nearly the same conditions.

The collection also includes *Dendrobium* from Southeast Asia and Vietnam that tolerate the same cool, humid conditions.

## Where they live

The cloud-forest *Dendrobium* of section *Oxyglossum* (cuthbertsonii, cyanocentrum, hellwigianum, victoriae-reginae, lawesii, and similar highland miniatures) are mounted on tree-fern plaques or cork bark near the **upper zone of the highland cabinet**, matching their semi-epiphytic habit at 2,000–3,000 m.

The lowland and Australian members (**'Berry Oda'**, **'Betty Goto' f. coerulea** and **D. speciosum**) do not belong in the cloud-forest regime. They live indoors in the living room on a bright windowsill, at intermediate conditions (~18–25 °C year-round, moderate humidity). 'Berry Oda' in particular flowers happily with nothing more than weekly watering.

## Cultivation notes

The single loss was **D. cuthbertsonii**, the most demanding *Oxyglossum* species. It was replaced with the **'Yellow'** clone from a different vendor, now a 5.5 cm fan that has made one new leaf per season for two years. The **'Blau' (blue) *cyanocentrum*** came in with one flower spike already on it and has repeat-bloomed every winter.

Several accessions (**D. speciosum**, **D. loddigesii**, **D. trantuanii**) have no recorded vendor or date, having come from growlists or trades; all have settled without trouble. **D. 'Berry Oda'**, an Australian hybrid of *D. kingianum* × *D. 'Mini Pearl'*, is the least fussy of the whole cluster and flowers heavily on new pseudobulbs with almost no input.

Overall survival is 11/12 (92 %). Miniature and highland *Dendrobium* do well in the cloud-forest cabinet provided species that need a dry rest (the *D. nobile* group, many Australian types) are kept out. The ones here all come from the fog zone rather than the seasonal monsoon zone, and that matters more than the genus name.
